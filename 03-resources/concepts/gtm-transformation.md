# GTM Transformation — What's Actually Broken

## The Challenge
"Don't just do the old way faster. Question whether deck creation is the real bottleneck vs. getting meetings scheduled."

This reframes the approach from "agentify presales steps" to "find and fix the actual conversion bottlenecks."

## Three Bottleneck Hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1: Stage 1→2 (Getting Meetings)
- If this is the bottleneck → the problem is BDR messaging relevance
- Generic outreach vs. research-backed messaging with specific data points
- Existing research skills become the *input* to BDR messaging, not to deck creation
- Need pipeline data to validate

### Hypothesis 2: Stage 3→4 (Meeting → Progress)
- Meetings happen but don't progress
- Missing piece: tying hypotheses to specific problem owners/personas
- Output isn't a deck — it's a "problem map" connecting quantified issues to named stakeholders

### Hypothesis 3: Stage 5→6 (Deal Stalling)
- Value case isn't strong enough or competitive positioning is weak
- Different tool entirely — deal intelligence copilot that enriches throughout the sales cycle

## The Fundamental Reframe: Continuous Account Intelligence

Instead of automating sequential presales steps (research → deck → demo → proposal), build a **persistent, compounding knowledge asset per account**.

Traditional presales = factory line. Knowledge evaporates after each meeting. Next person starts from scratch.

Account Intelligence System = every interaction enriches a persistent graph. Skills query and package that knowledge for specific moments.

## Architecture: Account Knowledge Graph

### Layer 1 — The Graph
Business objects: Account, Stakeholder, Problem/Hypothesis, Interaction, Signal, Use Case, Gap

**Account:** Industry, size, strategic priorities, tech landscape, competitive presence, health score
**Stakeholder:** Role in decision (buyer/champion/evaluator/blocker), KPIs, relationship strength, connections
**Problem Landscape:** Quantified business problems linked to owners. Confidence scores (validated by customer vs. hypothesised from public data)
**Interaction History:** Semantically rich — what landed, what didn't, what they pushed back on. Not just a CRM activity log.
**Signal Feeds:** Earnings transcripts, press releases, job postings, regulatory filings, leadership changes. A new CFO isn't just news — it triggers reassessment of budget ownership.

### Layer 2 — Intelligence Engine
- Signal interpretation (raw data → actionable intelligence)
- Gap analysis (what do we know vs. what do we need to know?)
- Hypothesis generation and scoring

### Layer 3 — Skills / Packaging
Existing skills (briefings, decks, demo scripts) become ways to *query and package* graph knowledge, not generate from scratch.

## Strategic Principles

1. The real bottleneck isn't deck creation — it's that account knowledge doesn't compound
2. Every presales engagement currently creates disposable artifacts, not reusable intelligence
3. The best AEs already work this way manually — the tool scales their approach to the whole org
4. This is also a competitive differentiator: "We understand your business better than any other vendor because our system learns from every interaction"

## The CRM Inversion

Traditional CRM: sellers do admin entry, data sits passively
Knowledge Graph: intelligence flows *out* to sellers. The system writes most notes automatically. Sellers confirm/correct.

Design principle: Sellers are validators, not data entry clerks. System should feel conversational.
